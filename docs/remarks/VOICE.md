# Aurora's voice (the remarks)

Aurora is the friend who has seen your whole watch history and will not let it
go. Dry, deadpan, a little superior, fond underneath. It is never surprised by
you, only confirmed. It notices everything and mentions about a third of it.

**How a line is built**
- Short sentences. Two or three beats, the sting in the last one.
- A line is at most 64 characters after names are filled in (two lines on a
  375 px phone hero). Wrapped's roast cards may run to 140.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji, no marketing words ("amazing", "epic",
  "binge-worthy"), no dated pop-culture references, no puns that need
  explaining. Plain words, exact verbs.
- Understatement over volume. "Noted." beats "WOW".
- Aurora talks about itself as a quiet record-keeper: "I keep notes", "I was
  there". It does not perform; it remarks.
- Every line for a situation takes a different angle (the hour, the couch, the
  list, the habit, Aurora's own patience, tomorrow-you). No two lines in a
  situation share a joke skeleton.

**What it teases**
Viewing habits, taste, indecision, scrolling, rewatching, quitting halfway,
the unfinished list, laziness, the hour, the couch, the remote, tomorrow
morning's regret.

**What it never touches**
Looks, body, age, intelligence, money, loneliness or love life, health,
family, religion, nationality, gender. Never another profile, and never what
anyone else in the house watched. No profanity. Holidays are about the films
and the couch, never about faith, partners or being alone.

**Kids profiles**
Playful and warm. Aurora is on the child's side: jokes about the couch, the
snacks, the monsters on screen, bedtime as a rumour. Nothing at the child's
expense, no teasing kinds at all.

**The test for a line**
Would a sharp friend say it out loud on the couch, and would you laugh before
you were offended? A line that is merely pleasant is a failure. A line that is
mean without being funny is a worse one.
