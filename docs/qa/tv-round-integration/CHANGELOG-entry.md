# Draft CHANGELOG.md entry for the TV round (paste at the top of CHANGELOG.md at release)

Not in CHANGELOG.md yet on purpose: the server reads every "## <version>" heading there into the app's What's new, so the entry goes in when the release sets the version. Replace `X.Y.Z` / the date; TV app version in the last bullet.

```
## X.Y.Z — 2026-10-DD

**The TV round: picture, sound, your phone and your voice.**

- **The screen follows the film.** In the Aurora TV app, a film made at 24 frames a second now plays at 24 (and 25, 50, 59.94 the same way), so slow pans glide instead of stuttering. The TV switches its refresh rate while the film plays and back when you leave; Settings → Picture and sound → Match frame rate turns it off (on the website: Settings → More settings → On the TV).
- **Dolby sound, as it is, and fewer server copies.** Each TV and browser tells the server what it can play, and the server decides once for both: films the device decodes play straight from the file, Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus and DTS go to the TV or soundbar as they are (5.1 kept instead of made stereo), and only what the device can't play is copied or converted. On the house's files that is every film direct on the TV instead of about half. Surround sound → Stereo only keeps everything stereo.
- **Night mode and dialogue boost**, in the player's ⚙ → Audio and in Settings → Sound, on the website and the TV: night mode brings loud bursts down and quiet voices up; dialogue boost makes voices clearer. Per person, on every screen. Not on iPhone, iPad or Safari (Apple's player keeps the sound to itself), and greyed on the TV while the sound is passed through to a receiver.
- **Subtitles your way.** Size, text colour, background (none, shadow, outline, box), box opacity, weight and position, chosen once for the person and shown the same on the TV and every browser — Subtitles → Subtitle style in the player, with a live sample, or Settings → Subtitles. Website subtitles are now bold by default, like the TV's.
- **Your phone is the remote.** A phone signed in to the same profile lists your TVs (and computers): swipe to move, tap for OK, a D-pad, pause, ±10/±30 s, scrub, subtitles and their timing, audio track, next episode, volume. **Play on TV** on title pages, episodes, the hold sheet and in the website player hands the film over at the same second with its subtitles and dub. An admin switch lets grown-ups steer the household's other TVs.
- **Search by voice.** The microphone in the website's search field (Chrome, Edge, Safari) and Voice on the TV's Search or the remote's microphone button: say a title in Hebrew or English — commands, "season two episode three" and spoken numbers are understood, and Hebrew names are looked up for their English title.
- **Home opens at once.** Home appears straight away with what you saw last time, on the website and the TV, and fresh news slips in without moving the row you are looking at.
- **Smoother on the TV.** Holding a direction glides through shelves and grids at an even 15 steps a second and stops where you let go; the words and bars on cards are drawn by the TV itself and come back after you leave a shelf; no card turns up late at the end of a row; the title page scrolls back up when you go up to its buttons; the billboard title has no faint box behind its shadow; changing the TV's display size or font while Aurora runs no longer leaves cut-off labels.
- My downloads (website and TV) tries again by itself after a failed read and says so ("Couldn't load your downloads — trying again…"), instead of "Nothing downloading yet" until a reload. A kids profile's empty Home says "Nothing for this age here yet". Names from the catalogue no longer show "&apos;" and similar codes.
- Fixes: in the classic look on a phone, the player's dock now fits on two rows (Picture in picture and Fullscreen were cut off) and the subtitle and settings menus stay inside the screen. Going Back while a title page was still loading could leave two screens on top of each other. Switching between profiles no longer makes requests the kids lock refuses. The photo viewer in a person's sheet no longer jumps back a photo on a slow phone. An admin's ticked episodes and open folders stay when the library changes underneath, and deleting a file the server is still reading waits for it instead of failing. Resync accepts the subtitle however the player names it.
- Server: a helper process (an encoder, a copy) that is stopped while the computer is busy now ends at once instead of holding its files and memory for up to a minute and a half.
- TV app TV-VERSION: all of the above on the TV.
```
